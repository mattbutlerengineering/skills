# Safe to translate, safe to hear

A rewrite that reads well in English and breaks in German, or looks right
and announces wrong, is not done. Sources: the Mailchimp Content Style
Guide's accessibility and translation pages, W3C WCAG 2.2 (labels and
accessible names), Vercel's web-interface-guidelines, and Impeccable's
`clarify` and `harden` commands (restated, not copied).

## Translation

- **Whole sentences only.** Never build a sentence from fragments
  (`"Delete " + count + " files"`). Word order differs by language; give
  the translator the whole sentence with named variables:
  `"Delete {count} files"`.
- **Plurals through the library.** Use the i18n library's plural forms
  (ICU `{count, plural, one {…} other {…}}` or its equivalent). Some
  languages have more than two forms; `count === 1 ? … : …` is wrong for
  them.
- **Variables keep their names.** A rewrite never renames, drops or
  reorders an interpolation variable's name, and never moves markup
  inside a variable.
- **Room to grow.** Translated text often runs a third longer than
  English, and short strings grow proportionally more. A label that only
  fits in English is a layout bug.
- **Right to left**, if the product supports it: no layout meaning
  carried by "left" and "right" in the copy.
- **Dates, numbers and currency** come from the locale formatter, never
  from string concatenation.
- **No idioms or wordplay** in strings that will be translated; they do
  not survive.

## Accessibility

- **The accessible name contains the visible label**, so a person using
  voice control can say what they see. An `aria-label` that differs from
  the visible text is a finding.
- **Alt text** describes what the image is for in context. A decorative
  image gets empty alt (`alt=""`), not a filename and not "image of".
- **No meaning carried by colour, icon or punctuation alone.** An error
  is said in words; a required field says "required".
- **No directional language**: "the button on the right" fails for a
  screen-reader user and on a phone.
- **Link and button text make sense out of context**, because screen
  readers list them out of context.
- **Announcements**: text that appears after an action (a toast, an
  inline error) is announced, through a live region, not only shown.
- **Abbreviations** are spelled out on first use.

## Verify on the rendered surface

- Every variable at its longest realistic value.
- The singular and the plural, and zero.
- A narrow viewport, and 200% browser zoom.
- A pseudo-locale or the longest shipped translation, if the product has
  one.
- Tab through it, and check the accessible names a screen reader reads.
