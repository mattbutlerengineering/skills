# The tells

A tell is a default nobody chose. Each one below is common enough that
people recognise an interface built from them at a glance — not as bad,
but as generic, and increasingly as machine-made.

**The test is never "is this pattern on the list".** It is: *was this
chosen for this product, and can you say why?* A typeface the brand
owns, a gradient the brief asks for, a card that really does group
related things: these are decisions, and decisions are not tells.
Swapping a default for the next most fashionable default is not a
decision either.

The practice of writing the tells down is Impeccable's; see
[`moves.md`](moves.md) for the credit. This is a short list written for
this pipeline, and it will age. Add to it when a new default shows up.

## Type

- **One default sans-serif for everything.** The face that ships with
  the framework, at the weights it ships with. *Decide* what the product
  should sound like and pick a face that sounds like it — or keep the
  system font on purpose, on a work surface where speed and familiarity
  matter more than voice.
- **Many sizes and no scale.** Fourteen, fifteen, sixteen, and seventeen
  pixels on one screen. *Decide* a scale with clear steps, and use fewer
  of them.
- **Hierarchy by weight alone.** Everything one size, some of it bold.
  *Decide* with size, space, and colour as well, and let the most
  important thing be much bigger.
- **Everything centred.** Centred paragraphs, centred lists. *Decide* to
  set what is read against the reading edge, and centre only short,
  isolated statements.
- **Lines too long to read.** Text running the width of a wide screen.
  *Decide* a measure of roughly 45 to 75 characters.

## Colour

- **The purple-to-blue gradient.** On the hero, the button, the logo
  mark. *Decide* a palette that comes from the product, its subject, or
  its brand — and usually flat colour.
- **Grey text on a coloured background.** It fails contrast and looks
  washed out. *Decide* a darker or lighter shade of the background's own
  hue.
- **Pure black and untinted grey.** *Decide* neutrals leaning slightly
  toward the brand hue, which read as intended rather than default.
- **Colour for decoration and for meaning at once.** Green that
  sometimes means success and sometimes means nothing. *Decide* which
  colours carry meaning, and reserve them for it.
- **A different accent on every component.** *Decide* one accent, used
  rarely enough to mean something.
- **A dark theme that is the light theme inverted.** *Decide* dark
  surfaces as their own design: gentler extremes, and depth shown by
  lightness rather than shadow.

## Layout

- **A card around everything, and cards inside cards.** *Decide* to
  group with space and alignment first. A container is for something
  that really stands apart.
- **The three-feature row.** An icon, a heading, two lines of text,
  three times over. *Decide* to show the product itself, or to say the
  one thing that matters at length.
- **The same page every time.** Hero, three features, testimonial,
  pricing, call to action. *Decide* the order and the form this
  product's argument actually needs.
- **Uniform spacing.** One gap between everything, so nothing is
  grouped. *Decide* tight within a group and generous between groups.
- **Everything at the same visual weight.** *Decide* what leads.

## Components and decoration

- **A rounded-square icon tile above every heading.**
- **Emoji standing in for icons.**
- **Gradient-filled text, glass blur, glow, and floating blobs,**
  applied because they are available.
- **A large radius and a soft shadow on every element.**
- **A badge or a pill on everything.**
- **A modal for every interaction,** including the ones that are not
  interruptions.
- **The sparkle icon wherever a model is involved.**

For all of these, *decide* whether the element carries information.
Decoration that carries none competes with the content that does.

## Motion

- **Bounce and elastic easing** on interface elements.
- **Everything fading in as the page scrolls.**
- **Animation as proof of effort:** hover effects on things that are not
  interactive.

*Decide* what change each animation explains. The motion move has the
rest.

## Copy

- **Claims instead of descriptions.** Seamless, powerful, revolutionary,
  effortless.
- **The interchangeable headline.** A sentence that would fit any
  product in the category.
- **"Get started" and "Learn more" on every button.**
- **An exclamation mark and a greeting on every screen.**
- **Invented proof.** A testimonial nobody gave, a wall of logos from
  customers the product does not have, a metric with no source. This one
  is not a matter of taste. It is a false statement made in the user's
  name. Never write it.

## Content and states

- **Placeholder content presented as real.** *Decide* real content, or
  content visibly marked as placeholder.
- **Only the happy path designed.** The empty list is a blank area and
  the error is one red sentence. The states move has the rest.
- **A spinner for every wait.** *Decide* to hold the layout with a
  skeleton, or to show nothing when the wait is short.
- **Perfect data in the mock-up.** Short names, round numbers, exactly
  three items.

## Using the list

- Before editing: which of these does the surface have, and which of
  them were chosen?
- After editing: which did you just add?
- An interface can avoid every entry here and still be bad. The list
  catches sameness. Critique catches everything else.
